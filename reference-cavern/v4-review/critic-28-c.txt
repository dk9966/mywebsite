**5/10 fulfillment.** The candidate preserves the station layout and blue industrial lighting, but the cavern reads as an architectural shaft rather than solid, excavated rock. It falls well short of 8.5.

1. **Rock masses look like tall panels with seams.** Walls and platform supports have long, flat vertical strips, with little convincing unequal subdivision or stepped excavation. Build substantial solid cores, carve connected ledges and recesses at several unequal scales, then add sparse corner chips. Keep broad matte faces; avoid scattered cube dressing.

2. **The camera and roof lack the reference’s enclosing depth.** The canvas proportions are close, but the candidate feels more frontal, with a thin ceiling edge and a large rectangular central shaft. Match the reference’s more downward-looking view and introduce thick, stepped ceiling overhangs, foreground rock framing, and irregular rock shoulders beneath platforms.

3. **Lighting emphasizes smooth walls instead of cavern depth.** The central beam illuminates a flat backing surface, while much of the surrounding structure disappears into black. Let the beam descend through open mist, add restrained cool fill to reveal rock silhouettes and stepped faces, and preserve localized warm workshop light. Depth should emerge through successive rock layers and atmospheric separation.
